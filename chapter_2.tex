\chapter{Related Works}

This chapter provides a synthesis of past research, existing algorithms, and state-of-the-art software that are related or similar to the thesis. It presents a cohesive comparison of different aspects of the works reviewed, focusing on approaches, application areas, and categories of solutions. The chapter aims to provide a deep understanding of the current state of the field, highlighting the challenges and opportunities relevant to the proposed research.

\section{Human-Chatbot Interaction}

Conversational agents, or chatbots, have been widely adopted across various fields such as mental health, education, and customer service, thanks to advancements in natural language processing (NLP). These AI-driven systems facilitate human-like interactions, offering real-time assistance. Research has demonstrated that chatbots can be especially valuable for users facing social isolation, discomfort in social situations, or difficulties accessing traditional support systems.

\subsection{Woebot}

Woebot is a chatbot designed to provide mental health support through cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)-based strategies. It engages users with daily check-ins, mood tracking, and psychoeducational content tailored to users' emotional states. A study by Fitzpatrick et al. (2017) evaluated Woebot's feasibility, acceptability, and efficacy for young adults experiencing depression and anxiety. The results showed a significant reduction in depressive symptoms, as measured by the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9), after two weeks of interaction. The study also found high user engagement, with an average of 12.14 interactions per participant during the study period. Key factors contributing to the chatbot's effectiveness included daily check-ins, empathy, and accountability. However, the study also pointed out limitations, such as repetitive responses and a lack of deep conversational understanding. To improve user engagement and effectiveness, the study emphasized the need for more personalized responses and better overall responsiveness.

\subsection{Chatbot to Enhance Social Skills of Security Guards}

In an experiment conducted for the security company "Workrate," de Bever et al. (2019) explored the use of chatbots to help security guards improve their social skills and practical exam performance. Two versions of a chatbot were developed: one that accepted both voice and text inputs and another that only accepted text. The chatbot simulated stress scenarios to help recruits practice their responses. Participants were divided into three groups: one group used the voice and text chatbot, another used the text-only version, and the third group underwent traditional learning methods. The study found no significant differences in exam scores between the groups, but the chatbot users reported a more engaging and encouraging learning experience. The positive reception suggested that chatbots can make training more interactive, but the study recommended further research with larger sample sizes to generate more meaningful statistical insights and develop engaging, user-retaining chatbots.

\subsection{Social Skill Training with Large Language Models}

A study proposed the use of large language models (LLMs) for social skills training through two AI models: the AI Partner and the AI Mentor. The AI Partner simulates human interactions, providing users with real-world experiential training, while the AI Mentor offers factual advice and personalized feedback. The combination of these two models is termed the APAM framework. The framework aims to create an effective learning environment for social skills development by following four key steps: understanding the social processes involved, designing an AI Partner to simulate these processes, developing an AI Mentor to provide tailored feedback, and integrating both into a simulated environment for iterative learning. While the framework has not yet been empirically tested, it highlights the potential of using LLMs for social skills training. However, challenges such as ensuring ethical and unbiased behavior, maintaining contextual awareness, and providing consistent, instructive feedback are noted. The authors suggest a gradual, risk-aware implementation of APAM, prioritizing human-centered design and formative evaluation.

\subsection{Development and Application of a Metaverse-Based Social Skills Training Program for Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder}

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a developmental condition characterized by difficulties in social interaction, speech, and nonverbal communication. Children with ASD often struggle with forming relationships, which can lead to social isolation and lifelong challenges. To address these issues, some researchers are exploring the use of digital technology, including metaverse-based programs, to improve social interaction skills in children with ASD. A randomized controlled trial protocol was developed to investigate the potential of a metaverse-based social skills training program. This approach aims to create an immersive, safe environment for children to practice social interactions and improve their ability to connect with peers. The study seeks to explore the feasibility and effectiveness of using virtual worlds to enhance social skills training for children with ASD.
