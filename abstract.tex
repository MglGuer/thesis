\begin{abstract}

Social communication can be a challenge for young adults, particularly in understanding implied meanings rather than explicit statements—a skill known as pragmatic competence. While artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots exhibit advanced conversational capabilities, existing educational tools often lack the conversational naturalism and culturally-aware evaluation frameworks necessary for effective social skills training. To address this gap, this study developed Socius, a dual-agent conversational AI system built upon the AI Partner-AI Mentor (APAM) framework. The system utilized the Google Gemini 3 API and a multi-pillar theoretical framework synthesizing Grice's Maxims, developmental psychology (Arnett and Havighurst), and Filipino cultural values (e.g., \textit{Pakikisama} and \textit{Hiya}). Socius facilitated realistic roleplay scenarios and generated quantitative, machine-parsable feedback enforced via JSON Schema Controlled Generation across three domains: Intention Coherence, Social \& Affective Nuance, and Communicative Effectiveness. A key architectural finding emerged during development: an attempt to fine-tune a smaller LLM (Mistral-7B) on a narrow pragmatics dataset resulted in overfitting and a loss of long-form coherence. This validated a successful pivot to sophisticated prompt-engineering with a generalist reasoning model, which yielded superior, contextually nuanced pedagogical feedback. Expert validation confirmed the theoretical and cultural relevance of the generated scenarios, and functional testing verified the system's end-to-end pipeline, including a "Holistic Mentor" for longitudinal progress tracking. By offering a robust, culturally-contextualized tool, this research contributes a novel methodology for AI-driven social communication training.
