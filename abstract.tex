\begin{abstract}

Social communication can be a challenge for young adults, particularly in understanding implied meanings rather than explicit statements. This is a skill namely known as pragmatic competence. While artificial intelligence (AI) 
chatbots exhibit advanced conversational capabilities, existing educational tools often lack the natural simulation of social situations, even more so the cultural backgrounds and values that are embedded in these situations, for personalized, 
contextually relevant communicative learning. As such, this study developed Socius, a dual-agent conversational AI system built upon the AI Partner-AI Mentor (APAM) framework. The system utilized the Google Gemini 3 API (Gemini 3-Flash) as the AI agents
for AI Partner conversation and AI Mentor feedback. This study also facilitated the development of a multi-pillar theoretical framework synthesizing Grice's Maxims, developmental psychology (Arnett and Havighurst) relevant for young adults, and Filipino cultural values (e.g., \textit{Pakikisama} and \textit{Hiya}) for 
the generation of conversational contexts to be used in roleplaying with the AI Partner and quantitative pragmatic assessment with qualitative feedback with the AI Mentor. From the generation of this framework came the a synthesized scoring framework to guide and score users'
pragmatic competence inspired by the PragmEval Framework. These components were all implemented as a coordinated system to provide a machine learning driven, personalized safe space for emerging adults to practice and develop their pragmatic competence. As such, these
different components were then evaluated through functional testing, expert validation and end-user testing with 24 emerging adults. This procedure resulted in the system working as intended through end-to-end testing, the expert validating the theoretical framework, and the end-users finding the system to be engaging, useful, and effective, yet in comparison created
a pedagogical paradox as the end users did not improve their pragmatic competence, based on the scoring framework, in a statistically significantly manner. Future work is recommended to address this through extended longitudinal evaluation periods, infrastructure stabilization, mandatory AI Mentor engagement, and expert validation of the scoring system's reliability to more conclusively measure Socius's pedagogical impact.

\end{abstract}
