\section{Background of the Study}

Social communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, facilitating relationships, emotional expression, and the exchange of ideas. However, for individuals who struggle with effective communication, whether due to social anxiety, neurodivergence, or general uneasiness in social settings, interacting with others can be challenging. Many individuals experience difficulties reading social cues, managing conversations, or expressing themselves confidently, which may lead to isolation or hinder personal and professional relationships.

The emergence of artificial intelligence (AI)-driven conversational technologies presents an opportunity to support individuals in enhancing their social communication skills. Chatbots are defined as computer programs designed to simulate human conversation, allowing users to interact with their digital devices as if they were conversing with a real person (Oracle, 2020). Notably, therapeutic chatbots such as Woebot have demonstrated effectiveness in providing emotional support and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), helping users navigate mental health challenges through structured conversations (Woebot Health, 2025).

A crucial element of effective social communication is empathy, which is defined by Merriam-Webster (n.d.) as the act or capacity to recognize, understand, and respond to the emotions of others. Emotional intelligence plays a fundamental role in fostering positive interactions by allowing individuals to navigate social complexities with sensitivity. Traditional chatbots often lack the ability to interpret emotions accurately, making it challenging to create meaningful conversations. Integrating sentiment analysis and tone recognition into chatbot design can improve their ability to respond with empathy, and as a result, improve user engagement and satisfaction.

In general, human interactions rely heavily on social cues such as body language, tone, and contextual understanding. Since chatbots primarily operate in a text-based environment, the absence of non-verbal cues must be compensated for by detecting markers in the user’s dialogue or linguistics that may convey emotion or intent. Advancements in natural language processing (NLP) and deep learning, particularly transformer-based models like GPT, have improved chatbots’ ability to interpret conversational context and generate contextually relevant responses (Pothuri, 2024). These models enable sentiment analysis, intent recognition, and emotion detection, allowing chatbots to adapt their responses to align with the user’s mood, intent, and expectations.

A chatbot’s ability to understand and maintain context throughout a conversation is essential for meaningful social interactions. Context awareness enables chatbots to recall past interactions, personalize responses, and provide continuity in conversations. Without proper contextual tracking, chatbot interactions may feel disjointed.

Despite the advancements in chatbot technology, existing conversational agents often lack the ability to facilitate meaningful, adaptive, and socially beneficial interactions. Most chatbots are designed for functional, task-oriented conversations rather than dynamic social changes that involve empathy, emotional intelligence, and nuanced responses. Furthermore, ethical concerns such as bias in chatbot responses, data privacy, and contextual misunderstanding are challenges to their widespread adoption for social communication purposes. Bias in AI models can lead to inappropriate or offensive interactions, affecting trust towards chatbot technology. For instance, if a chatbot is trained on biased data, it may inadvertently perpetuate stereotypes or discriminatory behavior (Zhou et al., 2024). Consequently, development in this field requires strategies such as bias detection and transparency in chatbot design to ensure fair and inclusive interactions. Additionally, ensuring user data privacy and security remains a priority, particularly for chatbots that engage in sensitive social interactions. Establishing clear data handling policies and employing security measures are essential to maintain user trust (Gujar, 2024).

This study explores the development of a one-on-one chatbot specifically designed to assist individuals in improving their social communication skills. Unlike task-based chatbots, this system aims to simulate natural conversations, providing users with real-time feedback, personalized responses, and opportunities to practice social interactions in a safe and judgment-free environment.
