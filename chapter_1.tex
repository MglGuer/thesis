\chapter{1. Introduction}\label{sec:intro}


\section{Background of the Study}
Social communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, facilitating relationships, emotional expression, and the exchange of ideas. However, for individuals who struggle with effective communication, whether due to social anxiety, neurodivergence, or general uneasiness in social settings, interacting with others can be challenging. Many individuals experience difficulties reading social cues, managing conversations, or expressing themselves confidently, which may lead to isolation or hinder personal and professional relationships. \hl{Thus, the definition of social communication is not limited by the mechanical production of speech or symbols, but rather the capacity to understand when, what, and how in communication to achieve effective and meaningful interactions between individuals}\ \cite{Hymes:1972, Light:1989}.


The emergence of artificial intelligence (AI)-driven conversational technologies presents an opportunity to support individuals in enhancing their social communication skills. Chatbots are defined as computer programs designed to simulate human conversation, allowing users to interact with their digital devices as if they were conversing with a real person (Oracle, 2020). Notably, therapeutic chatbots such as Woebot have demonstrated effectiveness in providing emotional support and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), helping users navigate mental health challenges through structured conversations (Woebot Health, 2025).

A crucial element of effective social communication is empathy, which is defined by Merriam-Webster (n.d.) as the act or capacity to recognize, understand, and respond to the emotions of others. Emotional intelligence plays a fundamental role in fostering positive interactions by allowing individuals to navigate social complexities with sensitivity. Traditional chatbots often lack the ability to interpret emotions accurately, making it challenging to create meaningful conversations. Integrating sentiment analysis and tone recognition into chatbot design can improve their ability to respond with empathy, and as a result, improve user engagement and satisfaction.

In general, human interactions rely heavily on social cues such as body language, tone, and contextual understanding. Since chatbots primarily operate in a text-based environment, the absence of non-verbal cues must be compensated for by detecting markers in the user’s dialogue or linguistics that may convey emotion or intent. Advancements in natural language processing (NLP) and deep learning, particularly transformer-based models like GPT, have improved chatbots’ ability to interpret conversational context and generate contextually relevant responses (Pothuri, 2024). These models enable sentiment analysis, intent recognition, and emotion detection, allowing chatbots to adapt their responses to align with the user’s mood, intent, and expectations.

A chatbot’s ability to understand and maintain context throughout a conversation is essential for meaningful social interactions. Context awareness enables chatbots to recall past interactions, personalize responses, and provide continuity in conversations. Without proper contextual tracking, chatbot interactions may feel disjointed.

Despite the advancements in chatbot technology, existing conversational agents often lack the ability to facilitate meaningful, adaptive, and socially beneficial interactions. Most chatbots are designed for functional, task-oriented conversations rather than dynamic social changes that involve empathy, emotional intelligence, and nuanced responses. Furthermore, ethical concerns such as bias in chatbot responses, data privacy, and contextual misunderstanding are challenges to their widespread adoption for social communication purposes. Bias in AI models can lead to inappropriate or offensive interactions, affecting trust towards chatbot technology. For instance, if a chatbot is trained on biased data, it may inadvertently perpetuate stereotypes or discriminatory behavior (Zhou et al., 2024). Consequently, development in this field requires strategies such as bias detection and transparency in chatbot design to ensure fair and inclusive interactions. Additionally, ensuring user data privacy and security remains a priority, particularly for chatbots that engage in sensitive social interactions. Establishing clear data handling policies and employing security measures are essential to maintain user trust (Gujar, 2024).

\section{Research Objectives}

This study aims to develop a one-on-one chatbot that helps young adults ages 15 to 24 years old improve their social communication skills. Unlike task-based chatbots, it will simulate natural conversations, provide real-time feedback, and offer a safe space for practicing social interactions. The objectives of the study are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To design and develop a chatbot that simulates natural, engaging, and socially beneficial one-on-one conversations aligned with principles of pragmatic competence.
    \item To integrate reflective feedback mechanisms that help users identify and improve their use of pragmatic strategies, including adherence to Grice’s Maxims and recognition of implicatures.
    \item To develop realistic contexts and situations that young adults are likely to encounter in academic, professional, and social environments, providing them with opportunities to practice and refine their pragmatic skills.
    \item To evaluate the chatbot’s effectiveness in enhancing users' pragmatic competence and social communication skills through user feedback and performance metrics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Limitations}

The study will limit its target demographic to those who are between 15 to 24 years old, aligning with the World Health Organization's ([WHO], n.d.) definition of youth. This age range was chosen as young adults often face crucial transitions in academic, social, and professional settings where effective communication plays a key role. \hl{The chatbot will also be limited to text-based input and will not incorporate voice or video-based input.} It will also be exclusively making use of the English language due to the availability of numerous data sets that may be used to train the chatbot. The study also aims to identify the different strategies used by experts in teaching communication skills to young adults, especially those who are deemed socially awkward. \hl{Furthermore, the chatbot itself will be the one to provide situations and contexts that the user will undergo, instead of the context being provided by the user. In accomplishing this, assistance strategies that pertain to helping young adults with social awkwardness, which may be used on a chatbot, are defined. Moreover, we'll be focusing on the response of the user themselves, rather than the way they respond. As such, this is to say we'll be prioritizing an individual's word choice rather than the manner they audibly speak their response}

\section{Significance of the Study}
While current chatbot applications focus more on therapeutic services, such as Woebot (Woebot Health, 2025), there is a lack of tools designed for non-therapeutic needs, such as for enhancing social communication skills. Moreover, Many young adults (15–24) struggle with real-world social interactions but lack accessible, judgment-free spaces to practice. Existing chatbots are not designed for adaptive social communication training, often failing to accommodate diverse communication styles or provide meaningful, real-time feedback. Thus, this study aims to provide a personalized, accessible platform for young adults who wish to improve their conversation skills.

Another significance of this research is its potential to benefit individuals within a specific age range. Being able to engage in conversation is a vital skill as it affects one’s personal relationships, casual and professional interactions, and self-confidence. By making use of a chatbot that simulates one-on-one conversations, users will be able to practice their communication skills in a low-pressure environment. This is particularly beneficial for those who struggle with face-to-face interactions, such as those with social anxiety or the socially awkward. 

Additionally, this study contributes to the reduction of bias in large language models (LLMs). Further review on the bias that affects the way LLMs interpret and respond to different communication styles, particularly those of neurodivergent individuals, can help improve their inclusivity and accuracy. By analyzing how LLMs process and adapt to diverse social behaviors, this study aims to refine chatbot interactions to be more accommodating to both neurotypical and neurodivergent users. This contributes to broader AI fairness efforts by ensuring that conversational AI tools do not reinforce stereotypes or exclude users with different communication preferences.
